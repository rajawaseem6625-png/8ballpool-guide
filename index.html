01 • AIMING

Think in lines, not luck.

The basic aiming routine

  1. Pick the pocket. Decide the exact pocket before choosing your cue-ball path.
  2. Find the contact point. Visualize where the cue ball needs to contact the object ball.
  3. Check the cue-ball path. Don't only ask whether the ball goes in — ask where the cue ball finishes.
  4. Set your speed. Smooth, controlled power is usually easier to repeat than a hard hit.
PRO TIP

Don't over-correct.

If a shot is slightly off, make a small adjustment. Large aim changes often create a second mistake.

02 • CUE CONTROL

Spin explained simply

Start with one type of spin at a time. Learn what the cue ball does after contact and after a cushion.

Top Spin

Encourages the cue ball to continue forward after hitting the object ball.

Back Spin

Can pull the cue ball back after object-ball contact. Useful for position play.

Left English

Changes the cue-ball's cushion reaction and can help shape difficult position shots.

Right English

Like left English, but mirrored. Use it intentionally rather than automatically.

03 • POSITION PLAY

Plan the next ball

1

Choose the pocket

Prefer a pocket and route that give you a comfortable next shot.

2

Choose your landing zone

Imagine a small area where the cue ball should finish, not just a single point.

3

Choose speed before spin

Ask whether simple speed can achieve the position before adding English.

4

Keep a safety option

If the run-out becomes low percentage, consider a defensive shot instead of forcing it.

04 • BANK SHOTS

Use the cushion deliberately

Start with one-rail banks

Practice sending an object ball to a cushion and then toward the pocket. Repeat from different distances and angles.

  • Start slowly.
  • Use the same table perspective.
  • Record which angles miss long or short.
  • Add spin only after your basic line is consistent.

FAQ

Common questions

Should beginners use spin on every shot?

No. Start with natural-ball and simple stop/follow shots. Add spin when it solves a specific positioning problem.

Why do I miss easy shots?

Common causes include rushing the stroke, changing aim at the last moment, excessive power, or poor cue alignment.

What should I practice first?

Straight shots, stop shots, controlled follow shots, and simple position play. These build the foundation for advanced shots.
